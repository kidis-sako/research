The Task

Given a pre-trained ResNet-50 model and the CIFAR-10 dataset, the challenge was to maximize classification accuracy under a strict constraint: only 5 training epochs. The key question: can automated hyperparameter tuning with Optuna push past the 85% accuracy barrier that manually chosen defaults fail to reach?

Approach: Optuna with TPE and Pruning

Optuna's Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) sampler was used to intelligently explore the hyperparameter space. Unlike grid or random search, TPE models the relationship between hyperparameters and performance, progressively focusing on promising regions.

To avoid wasting GPU time on poor configurations, a MedianPruner was enabled. After each epoch, Optuna compares a trial's intermediate accuracy against the median of completed trials; if it falls below, the trial is terminated early.

Why pruning matters: With 30 trials at 5 epochs each, pruning saved significant compute by killing underperforming trials before they finished all 5 epochs.

Search Space

Five hyperparameters were optimized simultaneously:

  • Batch size: 137 to 280 (integer)
  • Dropout rate: 0.0 to 0.25 (float)
  • Optimizer: RMSprop or Adam (categorical)
  • Learning rate: 1e-4 to 9e-4 (log-uniform)
  • LR scheduler: CosineAnnealingLR enabled/disabled (categorical)

The model itself was a ResNet-50 with pre-trained ImageNet weights, where only the final fully connected layer was replaced with a dropout + linear layer for 10-class CIFAR-10 output.

Results

Across 30 trials, Optuna found a configuration achieving 88.55% accuracy, well above the 85% target, in just 5 epochs:

Optimization history: TPE quickly finds a strong configuration (88.3%) in trial 1, then incrementally improves to 88.55% by trial 29. Only non-pruned trials are shown.

Best Configuration

  • Batch size: 150
  • Dropout rate: 0.205
  • Optimizer: RMSprop
  • Learning rate: 3.24e-4
  • Scheduler: CosineAnnealingLR enabled
Learning rate was king: Optuna's parameter importance analysis identified learning rate as the most influential hyperparameter by far, followed by batch size and dropout rate.

Key Findings

  • RMSprop consistently outperformed Adam on this transfer learning task: the best trials all used RMSprop, while some Adam trials were pruned early
  • CosineAnnealingLR always helped: every strong trial had the scheduler enabled, confirming that learning rate decay is critical when training for very few epochs
  • Moderate dropout (0.15--0.25) worked best. Too little led to slight overfitting, while higher values (explored in earlier iterations) degraded accuracy
  • Batch size around 150 struck the right balance between gradient noise (useful for generalization) and stable convergence

Takeaways

  • Automated HPO with Optuna can find configurations that outperform hand-tuned defaults: 88.55% vs. a baseline that failed to reach 85%
  • TPE is effective even with a modest budget (30 trials). It found a near-optimal region within the first few trials and refined from there
  • Pruning unpromising trials via MedianPruner is essential for making HPO practical on GPU-constrained budgets
  • Transfer learning + HPO is a powerful combination: pre-trained weights handle feature extraction while Optuna fine-tunes the training recipe
