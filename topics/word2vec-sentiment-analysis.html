The Task

Classify IMDB movie reviews as positive or negative using a self-trained Word2Vec embedding fed into a bidirectional LSTM. Rather than learning embeddings end-to-end inside the model, the goal was to first train Word2Vec on the review corpus, then freeze those embeddings as a non-trainable layer in the LSTM, a form of transfer learning for NLP.

Preprocessing Pipeline

Raw movie reviews required substantial cleanup before they could be used for embedding training and classification:

  • Lowercasing and removal of all non-alphabetic characters
  • Stop word removal using NLTK's English stopwords corpus
  • WordNet lemmatization to reduce words to their base verb forms (e.g., "murdered" → "murder", "committed" → "commit")
Why lemmatize? Reducing inflected words to a common base form shrinks the vocabulary and lets Word2Vec learn stronger associations from a fixed-size corpus.

Word2Vec Embedding

A gensim Word2Vec model was trained on the full 50,000-review corpus with the following configuration:

  • Algorithm: CBOW (Continuous Bag of Words)
  • Vector size: 20 dimensions
  • Window: 5 tokens
  • Min count: 1 (include all words)
  • Vocabulary: 86,547 unique tokens

The trained embeddings captured meaningful semantic relationships:

Similar to "good": decent (0.87), great (0.86), alright (0.85), nice (0.82), ok (0.82)

Similar to "horrible": terrible (0.98), awful (0.97), bad (0.92), pathetic (0.89), dreadful (0.88)
Semantic quality: Even with only 20 dimensions, Word2Vec learned that "horrible" and "terrible" are near-synonyms (0.975 cosine similarity), a strong signal for sentiment classification.

Model Architecture

The sentiment classifier consists of:

  • Embedding layer: 86,548 × 20, initialized with Word2Vec weights, frozen (no gradient updates)
  • Bidirectional LSTM: 64 hidden units per direction (128 total output)
  • Fully connected layer: 128 → 1 with sigmoid activation for binary sentiment

By freezing the embedding layer, the LSTM only needs to learn how to combine the pre-trained word representations for classification, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters.

Training and Results

The model was trained for 5 epochs on GPU with binary cross-entropy loss:

Training curves show an initial validation spike in epoch 2 followed by recovery. By epoch 5, both train and validation accuracy converge near 86%.

Test accuracy: 87.01%

The validation loss spike at epoch 2 is notable: the model temporarily overfits before the LSTM finds a more generalizable mapping from the frozen embeddings. This is characteristic of transfer learning setups where the feature extractor is fixed.

Live Predictions

Testing the trained model on custom review snippets reveals how the bidirectional LSTM interprets sentiment through its frozen Word2Vec lens. The output is the probability of a positive review (>0.5 = positive, <0.5 = negative):

Input Text P(positive)
This movie was absolutely fantastic and heartwarming 0.912
An awful plot and terrible acting made it a waste of time 0.008
Not bad, but could have been better 0.457
This movie was so bad and good at the same time, I can't decide. I really don't think it was a bad movie at all! 0.128
It would be a lie to say I hate this movie 0.630
I think this movie should get an Oscar 0.704
I hate this movie but it should get an Oscar 0.695
I absolutely hate this movie but I absolutely love it too 0.635

Clear-cut reviews are classified with high confidence ("fantastic" → 0.91, "awful" → 0.01). Ambiguous inputs like "Not bad, but could have been better" land near the decision boundary (0.46). Interestingly, the model struggles with negation and mixed sentiment: "I hate this movie but it should get an Oscar" is classified as positive (0.70), likely because "Oscar" carries strong positive signal in movie review embeddings.

Negation is hard: The model reads "hate" + "love" in "I absolutely hate this movie but I absolutely love it too" and leans positive (0.63), suggesting the LSTM weighs later tokens more heavily through its recurrent state.

Takeaways

  • Self-trained Word2Vec embeddings on domain-specific text (movie reviews) produce high-quality semantic representations even at 20 dimensions
  • Freezing embeddings as a non-trainable layer is an effective transfer learning strategy that reduces training time and prevents overfitting on small datasets
  • A bidirectional LSTM achieves 87% test accuracy on IMDB sentiment, demonstrating that compact Word2Vec embeddings carry sufficient signal for binary classification
  • Preprocessing matters: stop word removal and lemmatization directly improved embedding quality by reducing vocabulary noise
