The Challenge

Custom prosthesis design traditionally relies on manual measurements of a patient's residual limb by orthopedic technicians. This process is time-consuming and introduces variability between practitioners.

An alternative approach worth exploring: combining ultrasound imaging with deep learning to automatically identify bone and tissue structures, potentially making prosthesis design faster, more accurate, and more consistent.

The 2D+t Approach: Time as the Depth Dimension

Instead of processing ultrasound frames independently, this project implements a 2D+t temporal approach where video sequences are treated as 3D volumes:

  • X-axis: Image width
  • Y-axis: Image height
  • Z-axis (depth): Time dimension (frame sequence)

This spatiotemporal representation allows the network to learn from neighboring frames during training, capturing both spatial bone structures and temporal motion patterns.

Why 2D+t? Treating time as a spatial dimension enables the model to learn temporal patterns, leading to more stable predictions.

Key advantages of the 2D+t approach:

  • Temporal consistency: Predictions remain stable across consecutive frames, eliminating flickering artifacts
  • Context-aware segmentation: The network sees neighboring frames, improving accuracy in challenging regions
  • Motion pattern learning: Captures how bone structures appear during probe movement
  • Clinical realism: Mirrors the continuous nature of real ultrasound examinations

Implementation: nnU-Net with 3D Temporal Architecture

nnU-Net v2 serves as the backbone architecture, configured for 3D spatiotemporal processing:

  • Self-configuring 3D U-Net architecture optimized for temporal ultrasound data
  • 3D convolutions capture spatiotemporal features across frame sequences
  • Custom preprocessing pipeline: videos converted to 3D NIfTI volumes (H × W × T)
  • Temporal spacing calibrated to actual ultrasound frame rate (20 FPS)
  • Spatial spacing derived from probe calibration (0.054 mm/pixel)
Technical Implementation: Each video becomes a single 3D training case, improving temporal coherence.

Data Pipeline: From Video to 3D Volumes

The 2D+t approach requires a specialized data conversion pipeline:

Video to 3D NIfTI Conversion:

  • Extract all frames from ultrasound video sequences
  • Stack frames chronologically to form 3D volumes (Height × Width × Time)
  • Set proper voxel spacing: spatial (0.054 mm/pixel) and temporal (based on 20 FPS)
  • Save each video as a single 3D NIfTI file for training

Training Dataset:

  • Foundation data: UltraBones100k dataset with 100,000+ labeled ultrasound frames across diverse anatomies
  • Project-specific data: Custom collected video sequences from residual limb scans
  • Dataset structure: 14 cadaver specimens with multiple scan trajectories per limb
Data Transformation: The 2D+t method organizes data as temporal video volumes instead of independent frames.

Qualitative improvements observed:

  • Temporal stability: Smooth, flicker-free segmentation masks across consecutive frames
  • Motion coherence: Network understands bone movement patterns during probe scanning

Clinical Impact

The 2D+t approach enables temporal consistency essential for 3D reconstruction from tracked ultrasound sweeps. This provides automated, reproducible measurements for improved prosthesis fit and accuracy.

Future: Integration with probe tracking for full 3D bone reconstruction and multi-class tissue segmentation.
